📊 Discussion on Current Evaluation Limitations

Current evaluation protocols use Hungarian matching to align predictions with ground truth before computing metrics (IoU w/ Hungarian). While this ensures optimal matching, it masks a critical issue: False Positives.

⚠️ Problem:

  • Real-world applications have no ground truth
  • False positives (extra detections) degrade downstream tasks (such as 4D reconstruction)
  • Hungarian matching "hides" these false positives in evaluation
  • Methods like Flow-SAM (SOTA method) may have high IoU but many false positives

🎯 Better Way:

  • Current evaluation omits false positives, so we also report a metric (IoU w/o Hungarian) that is computed without Hungarian matching

✨ Our Advantage:

  • Our method produces fewer false positives
  • Better suited for practical usage
  • IoU w/o Hungarian captures this benefit, and shows other methods' redundant false positives

📊 Dataset Annotation Challenges for Motion Segmentation

Motion segmentation faces unique challenges compared to video object segmentation, particularly in terms of dataset annotation quality and coverage.

🤔 Motion Segmentation (MS) vs Video Object Segmentation (VOS):

  • Motion Segmentation seeks to automatically identify and segment objects that are moving in world coordinates, even under camera motion
  • Video Object Segmentation focuses on tracking specific objects of interest, regardless of their motion status
  • This fundamental difference requires different annotation strategies

⚠️ Dataset Scarcity:

  • High-quality datasets specifically annotated for motion segmentation are scarce
  • Most existing approaches are forced to rely on VOS datasets (e.g., DAVIS) for training and evaluation
  • This mismatch between dataset design and task requirements creates systematic issues

⚠️ Incomplete Annotations in VOS Datasets:

  • VOS datasets label salient or interesting objects, not necessarily all moving objects
  • As shown in the examples below, many truly moving objects are left unlabeled
  • This creates ambiguity: should a model predict only labeled objects or all moving objects?

⚠️ Consequences:

  • Supervised MS methods trained on VOS data tend to behave more like salient object segmentation
  • They learn to predict "interesting" objects rather than genuinely moving objects
  • This deviation from true motion segmentation limits their practical applicability

📊 What Constitutes "Motion" in Motion Segmentation?

Defining "motion" is more nuanced than it initially appears. While our epipolar geometry approach can detect most form of motion in world coordinates, not all detected motion is relevant for practical applications.

🌍 General Motion Definition:

  • Physical Motion: Any change in world coordinates, detectable through epipolar geometry
  • This includes: moving vehicles, walking people, animals, but also water ripples, shadows, reflections, etc.
  • Our epipolar error inherently captures all these motions without discrimination

🎯 Task-Specific Motion (Practical Definition):

  • In downstream tasks like 4D reconstruction, we care about rigid or articulated objects
  • Not all physical motion is relevant: water surfaces, shadows, reflections are typically excluded
  • Our method adopts a practical definition aligned with real-world applications

⚠️ Challenge 1: Epipolar Error Captures Everything

  • Our epipolar-based approach can detect all motion patterns, including irrelevant ones
  • Examples: floating water surfaces, moving shadows, reflections on glossy surfaces
  • These are "motion" in the geometric sense, but not "objects" we want to segment

⚠️ Challenge 2: SAM's Limitation on Non-Objects

  • SAM is designed to segment objects, not abstract motion patterns
  • When prompted with a shadow point on a road, SAM segments the entire road, not the shadow
  • When prompted with water ripples, SAM struggles to define consistent boundaries
  • These are not SAM's failures—they're simply outside its designed scope

✅ Our Solution: Heuristic Filtering

  • To better serve real-world applications, our stage-3 heuristic filtering automatically removes irrelevant motion patterns, such as shadows, water ripples, and reflections, which typically appear in the background.
  • This process ensures that only truly meaningful moving objects—like vehicles and people—are retained for downstream tasks such as 4D reconstruction.
